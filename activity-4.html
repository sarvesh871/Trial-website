1 Objective & Learning Outcomes

Objective: To build a multi-year financial model for a startup that demonstrates unit economics, the break-even point at scale, and the capital requirements needed to reach that scale.

Learning Outcomes

  • Construct unit economics (CAC, LTV, contribution margin) for a chosen venture.
  • Build a 3–5 year revenue, cost, and cash-flow projection.
  • Determine the break-even volume/timeline and the associated capital requirement.

2 Background / Theory

Investors and founders alike evaluate scalability through the lens of unit economics — does each additional unit sold or customer acquired generate more value than it costs? This exercise builds a financial model from first principles: unit-level revenue and cost, scaled to company-level projections, to determine when and at what capital cost the venture becomes self-sustaining.

The Contribution Margin Method for break-even analysis isolates fixed overhead from per-unit variable costs:

Contribution Margin (CM) = Unit Price (P) − Unit Variable Cost (VC)
Break-Even Volume (Units) = Total Fixed Overhead ÷ CM
Break-Even Revenue = Break-Even Volume × Unit Price

3 Materials / Resources Required

  • Spreadsheet software: Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets.
  • Financial modelling template: Prescribed in Annexure D.
  • Assumed/researched cost & pricing data: For the chosen product category (e.g. Solvanta PowerBlock™).

4 Procedure / Tasks

  1. 1
    Define product & volume: Define the product/service, pricing, and target customer volume by year for years 1 through 5.
  2. 2
    Compute unit economics: Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), Customer Lifetime Value (LTV), contribution margin per unit, and the LTV:CAC ratio.
  3. 3
    Build cost structure: Separating fixed costs (facility lease, salaried engineering/admin staff, equipment depreciation) from variable costs (materials BOM, direct contract assembly labor, logistics per unit).
  4. 4
    Project annual P&L: Project revenue, gross margin, operating expenses (OpEx), and EBITDA for each year.
  5. 5
    Identify break-even point: In both units sold and elapsed time, showing the calculation method (contribution margin approach).
  6. 6
    Estimate total capital requirement: Calculate capital required to reach break-even (covering cumulative negative cash flow) and propose a balanced funding structure (equity / debt / non-dilutive grants).
  7. 7
    Build sensitivity table: A simple sensitivity table showing the impact of ±20% price and ±20% CAC on break-even timing.

5 Deliverables / Submission (Annexure D Model)

Your lab submission must include:

  • Unit economics summary (CAC, LTV, contribution margin, LTV:CAC).
  • 5-year P&L and cash-flow projection spreadsheet.
  • Break-even analysis with complete supporting calculations.
  • Capital requirement estimate and funding structure recommendation.
  • Sensitivity analysis table.
